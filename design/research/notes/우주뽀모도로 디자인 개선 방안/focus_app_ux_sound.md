# Focus-App UX, Motivation, Ambient Design, and Attention Evidence (for the Cosmodoro redesign)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Sources are mostly App Store listings, product sites, third-party reviews, and peer-reviewed papers (often seen through abstracts or summaries). Marketing-only claims are labeled. Reddit threads did not come up in searches, so user sentiment below comes from App Store review text, Trustpilot, and review sites.

## 1. Leading focus apps: core loops, rituals, rewards, streaks, stats, social, monetization, review sentiment

### Takeaway
The apps that stick pair a simple core loop (start timer → visible thing grows or travels → it survives only if you stay) with a long-term collection: a forest, a flight log, a pet, or a ranking. The strongest differentiators are real stakes (the tree dies, or the whole group's tree dies), honest records (failed sessions stay visible), and, in Korea, social presence and rankings. Recurring complaints are paywalls on basics, redesigns that bury the timer and daily total, integrity problems in rankings, and bugs and crashes after updates.

### Cited Findings

**Blockers and gamified accountability (Opal, Habitica)**

**Streak mechanics**
- Duolingo openly uses loss aversion. Its PM argued streak freezes keep fear of losing a streak from stopping people starting one. Reportedly, learners with "intense" daily goals were less likely to keep streaks, and the company concluded that lowering barriers mattered more than daily volume. These figures could not be verified against Duolingo's original data. Some users use freezes 1–2× per week. — [CMU UXA analysis](https://cmu-uxa.notion.site/Performative-Progress-Strava-Mules-Duolingo-Streaks-1c18eb2b3ca480f59cd2e4123ac0d7d2)
- Streaks can create anxiety and compulsive use. Suggested mitigations include weekly rather than daily streaks. — [Smashing Magazine 2026](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-streak-system-ux-psychology/)

### Inferences
- The core loop Cosmodoro needs is "boarding pass → launch → cruise → arrival → log entry". FocusFlight and Orbital Focus already prove the loop works; what sets an app apart is depth of the collection (planet passport, cumulative journey) and session integrity (honest handling of aborted flights), not the 3D view itself.
- Forest's "failed sessions stay visible" and "no shame language" suggest that an aborted flight should be logged as a "returned to Earth" or "emergency landing" entry, not deleted. It should also never be punished with lost progress beyond that one flight.
- Habitica's critique is a warning: keep the game layer thin and tied to real focus minutes. No mini-games or boss fights that compete with the task.
- Monetization patterns that don't erode focus: cosmetics (ship skins, planet themes, sound packs), advanced stats, and no ads during sessions (Forest's explicit rule). A web app can't use Screen Time, so "blocking" has to be soft. For example, detecting tab hidden or blur through the Page Visibility API and logging it as "turbulence".
- Group stakes ("if one gives up, everyone's tree dies") are a strong social mechanic for Korean study groups. Make it opt-in, because it's harsh.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or r/GetStudying threads surfaced, so I have no direct quotes on what Reddit users praise or criticize.
- Structured, Be Focused, and Flow (Mac) weren't covered by results, so I have no verified feature or pricing data for them.
- No first-party FocusFlight review text (praise or complaints) was retrievable. The AppFollow page timed out on direct fetch.
- No verified retention numbers for any app's streak or collection mechanics.

## 2. Ambient sound design for focus: noise colors, binaural beats, generative/adaptive audio, cabin hum, Web Audio

### Takeaway
The best evidence says that for most people, lyrics and intelligible speech hurt reading and focus, while instrumental music is roughly neutral. White and pink noise give a small benefit to people with ADHD (g≈0.25) and a small cost to people without ADHD (g≈−0.21). Brown noise is completely unstudied, and binaural-beat entrainment evidence is inconsistent. Brain.fm's amplitude-modulation findings are promising but co-authored by the company. Endel's adaptive claims are marketing-only. Sound should be optional, defaulted off or soft, and described honestly.

### Cited Findings
- **Nigg et al. 2024 meta-analysis (JAACAP 63(8):778–788):** 13 studies (N=335) found a small but significant benefit of white or pink noise on lab attention tasks for ADHD or high-symptom groups (g=0.249, 95% CI 0.135–0.363), with GRADE certainty rated moderate. Across 11 studies, non-ADHD participants showed a negative effect (g≈−0.21; abstract and main text differ slightly). Twelve studies used white noise and one used pink; no brown-noise studies exist. Caveats: participants weren't blinded, exposures were only minutes long, dB levels varied, and sustained loud exposure risks hearing damage. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/); [PsyPost coverage](https://www.psypost.org/white-and-pink-noise-show-promise-in-enhancing-attention-in-those-with-adhd/)
- The British Psychological Society's research digest asks whether pink noise "really" helps focus. Its secondary framing: missing brown-noise data means "we don't know yet", not "it doesn't work". — [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/does-pink-noise-really-help-you-focus); [Flinders blog](https://blogs.flinders.edu.au/student-health-and-well-being/?p=6480)
- **Background music and reading:** a Bayesian meta-analysis (Vasilev, Kirkby & Angele, Perspectives on Psychological Science 2018) found background noise, speech, and music all have a small but reliable negative effect on reading. Intelligible speech and lyrical music are the most distracting. Secondary summaries of that work give an overall d≈−0.19, lyrical music d≈−0.35, and instrumental ≈0. An earlier Kämpfe et al. 2011 meta-analysis found an overall null effect, plausibly because positive and negative effects canceled out. — [Perspectives on Psych Science](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691617747398/); [Bournemouth eprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/); [Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/273)
- Background music with English lyrics impaired Chinese reading performance in a 2022 study, so foreign-language lyrics still distract. — [Bournemouth preprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/38543/1/Preprint_rev1.pdf)
- **Binaural beats:** a 2023 systematic review (Ingendoh et al., PLoS ONE) covered 14 EEG studies. Five supported brainwave entrainment, eight contradicted it, and one was mixed, with very heterogeneous protocols. A 2023 meta-analysis on memory and attention describes the literature as "mixed". — [PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/)
- An umbrella review reports benefits from 40 Hz binaural beats on cognition in Alzheimer's and related diseases. That is a clinical population, so it doesn't generalize to healthy students. — [PMC12760695](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12760695/)
- **Brain.fm / Northeastern (Communications Biology, Oct 2024):** music with fast, strong amplitude modulation, reportedly in the beta range (12–20 Hz), improved sustained attention compared with slow-modulation music and pink noise. Higher-ADHD-symptom participants benefited more. fMRI and EEG (about 40 participants) showed greater attention-network activity and tighter stimulus-brain coupling. It was NSF-funded, but Brain.fm scientists co-authored it (conflict of interest). — [Northeastern News](https://news.northeastern.edu/?p=251143); [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/background-music-amplitude-modulation-adhd-study/)
- **Endel:** adaptive inputs are time of day, weather, light, heart rate, and location. The company says the iOS app lowers BPM when heart-rate spikes persist. The "7× focus / 3.6× less stress" figures come from the company, with no independent publication found. — [endel.io](https://endel.io/); [Amazon Science](https://amazon.science/latest-news/the-science-behind-endels-ai-powered-soundscapes); [Basic Apple Guy](https://basicappleguy.com/basicappleblog/endel)
- **Cabin hum in flight apps:** FocusFlight ships "airplane ambience" next to focus music. No study found on cabin or engine noise as a focus aid. — [AppFollow listing](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/focusflight-deepfocus-timer/6648771147?country=it)
- **Web Audio implementation:** brown noise can be generated by passing white noise through a leaky integrator (Zach Denton's method), and pink noise with Paul Kellet's refined −3 dB/octave filter. — [Software Mansion noise guide](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-audio-api/docs/guides/noise-generation)
- An AudioWorklet runs on the audio rendering thread: `audioWorklet.addModule()`, then `new AudioWorkletNode(ctx, 'name')`. That is the modern place to put the noise generators. — [MDN AudioWorkletProcessor](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioWorkletProcessor)
- Existing open-source examples: NoiseGenJS (brown, white, pink via Web Audio, pre-worklet) and Megaemce/Oscillator (an AudioWorkletProcessor with white, pink, and brown noise). — [NoiseGenJS](https://github.com/ichabodcole/NoiseGenJS); [Megaemce/Oscillator](https://github.com/Megaemce/Oscillator)

### Inferences
- Default sound design for Cosmodoro: "cabin hum" built from procedurally generated brown or pink noise through a low-pass filter (roughly 200–600 Hz cutoff for an engine-like rumble). Add slow, subtle gain LFOs (seconds-scale, not beat-scale) and optional sparse "radio chatter-free" textures. Never use intelligible voices or lyrics during cruise. This follows the speech and lyrics distraction evidence.
- Offer a clear "silence" option and keep defaults at low volume. Non-ADHD users may perform worse with noise, so sound should be opt-in or easy to mute, with a hearing-safety hint.
- Avoid marketing binaural "brainwave" claims. If a binaural mode exists, label it "experimental / preference". An amplitude-modulated music mode can cite the 2024 study, with the conflict of interest noted.
- Tie sound cues to state changes: departure rumble swell, gentle chime at arrival, and soft "cabin bell" at break start. This mirrors Brain.fm's audible interval cue. Keep cues short and non-startling.
- Per-planet ambient variants (Mars = dusty low wind, Neptune = deep hum) can add identity without adding lyrics or speech.

### Gaps
- The pooled result of the 2023 meta-analysis on binaural beats, memory, and attention (Psychological Research) wasn't retrieved. A frequently cited 2019 meta-analysis reporting g≈0.45 for binaural beats appeared only in search summaries, and I couldn't verify the primary paper.
- No peer-reviewed study on brown noise, engine or cabin noise, or "generative adaptive" soundscapes for focus in healthy adults.
- The Brain.fm paper's exact effect sizes and sample sizes per experiment weren't verified from the original article.
- No safe-dB guidance specific to focus apps was found beyond the Nigg caveat.

## 3. Visuals and attention: animated/3D backgrounds, nature/space imagery, soft fascination, ART, motion and load, dark mode, reduced motion

### Takeaway
Motion is the strongest pull on attention in the periphery, and animated content measurably disrupts reading (more and longer fixations, more regressions). The evidence that on-screen nature imagery restores attention is real but weaker than real nature, and sustained-attention benefits are modest. Dark mode looks good, but dark-on-light text gives better proofreading performance. Accessibility guidance (WCAG 2.3.3) explicitly flags parallax and non-essential motion. For Cosmodoro, the 3D flight view should be a slow, low-contrast "window" that is mostly still during work, with motion reserved for transitions and a reduced-motion mode.

### Cited Findings
- **Motion captures attention:** motion outperforms color and shape as a cue "especially in the periphery". Some motion types are "inherently much more distracting and irritating than others". — [Bartram et al. (Interact 2001)](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Bartram-Interact01.pdf)
- **Animated ads and reading:** in eye-tracking, increasingly animated ads caused more and longer fixations and more regressions on the text than static ads, and flashing text interfered with word processing. — [Lan, Liao & Kruger, via CiteDrive](https://citedrive.com/en/discovery/do-advertisements-disrupt-reading-evidence-from-eye-movements)
- **Far-peripheral motion:** in a study of 39 adults, the cognitive-load effect wasn't statistically significant overall, but men spent less time on the test under peripheral motion. The effects are small and inconsistent. — [Bevilacqua, Paas & Krigbaum (UOW)](https://ro.uow.edu.au/sspapers/2560)
- **Text over moving backgrounds:** background motion along reading directions produced less discomfort than other directions. Reading time and saccades were unaffected, and the authors attribute discomfort to arousal. — [Swiss J. Psychology (Hogrefe)](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1024/1421-0185.63.3.183)
- **Peripheral displays:** one study found animated textual peripheral displays did not distract from a central browsing task. Distraction split into "display-driven" and "self-interruption", and animation affected only the former. — [Georgia Tech Interact 2001 paper](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~john.stasko/papers/interact01.pdf)
- **Attention Restoration Theory (Stevenson, Schilhab & Bentsen 2018 meta-analysis, 46 studies):** nature exposure improved working memory, attentional control, and cognitive flexibility, with low-to-moderate effects. Actual exposure produced different (larger) effects than virtual exposure (on-screen images), partly because real exposures lasted longer. Sustained attention and vigilance weren't among the improved domains. — [NAAEE eePRO summary](https://eepro.naaee.org/research/eeresearch/attention-restoration-theory-ii-systematic-review-clarify-attention-processes)
- **Brief images:** a 2022 study using 40-second nature images found only a modest sustained-attention effect, with mechanisms "unclear". — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.809629/full)
- A 2025 EEG study (n=63) tested 10 minutes of nature vs urban imagery on error-related negativity in a flanker task. Results weren't visible in my sources. — [Frontiers Hum. Neurosci. 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12209298/)
- **Dark mode:** Piepenbrock, Mayr & Buchner found a positive-polarity (dark text on light) advantage in proofreading for both younger and older adults. A 2014 follow-up found smaller pupils and better proofreading with positive polarity, attributed to display luminance. Many users still prefer dark screens to avoid glare. — [Fundación MAPFRE record 2013](https://documentacion.fundacionmapfre.org/documentacion/publico/pt/bib/143702.do?format=xml); [Fundación MAPFRE record 2014](https://documentacion.fundacionmapfre.org/documentacion/publico/en/bib/150136.do); [NN/g Dark Mode](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/); [cogsci.nl](https://cogsci.nl/blog/is-bright-text-on-a-dark-background-a-good-idea)
- **WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) Animation from Interactions:** non-essential motion triggered by interaction must be disableable, and parallax is named as a common non-essential animation. Options include avoiding it, an on/off toggle, or honoring the OS reduce-motion setting. — [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/animation-from-interactions.html); [Deque](https://dequeuniversity.com/resources/wcag2.1/2.3.3-animations-from-interactions)
- **Implementation pattern:** wrap parallax or motion in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` with a static default, and listen for runtime changes in JS. — [web.dev prefers-reduced-motion](https://web.dev/prefers-reduced-motion); [Front-End Checklist](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/accessibility/parallax-effects)
- Vestibular accommodation guidance (University of Michigan) recommends avoiding large-scale motion for users with vertigo or vestibular disorders. — [UMich KB](https://teamdynamix.umich.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/Article/14019/Accommodating-Vertigo-and-Vestibular-Disorders)

### Inferences
- The flight view should be a "cabin window": slow drift (stars barely moving), low contrast, and no sudden events during cruise. Save camera moves, burns, and planet reveals for launch, break, and arrival transitions, where capturing attention is the goal. A "minimal cruise" toggle could fade the 3D scene to near-static after 30–60 seconds, or show it only when the user glances at the tab.
- The work surface (todo list, current task) should not sit over a moving background. Use a solid or blurred panel. Animated content next to text increases fixations and regressions.
- Space imagery isn't "nature" in ART terms, and the evidence for screen-based restoration is modest. Use calm, vast imagery mainly in breaks ("observation deck"), not as an active focus enhancer, and don't claim restoration benefits.
- Offer both dark (cockpit) and light themes. If the todo list or stats involve real reading, give them a higher-luminance surface or a light option.
- Ship a reduced-motion mode that honors `prefers-reduced-motion` by default: static starfield, crossfades instead of camera flights, no parallax. Also add an in-app toggle (WCAG 2.3.3).
- On the web, GPU-heavy 3D also costs battery and fan noise on laptops, a hidden distraction. Consider throttling the render loop (for example, 15–30 fps during cruise and pausing when the tab is hidden).

### Gaps
- The 2023 Experimental Psychology study "Does Watching Videos With Natural Scenery Restore Attentional Resources?" was paywalled (HTTP 403), so I couldn't confirm its result.
- No study directly tests 3D space-flight backgrounds or "soft fascination" in productivity UIs. Kaplan's soft-fascination concept appears in the ART literature, but no app-specific evidence was found.
- No retrievable data on what share of users enable reduced motion (an unverified "35% of adults over 40 have vestibular disorders" claim on a blog should not be used).

## 4. Session design patterns: breaks (25/5, 50/10, flowtime), arrival rituals, reflection, focus ratings, distraction logging, DND and blocking

### Takeaway
Systematic breaks (Pomodoro-style) gave similar task completion in less time, with lower fatigue and higher motivation than fully self-regulated breaks in one study. A 2025 follow-up found no overall differences between Pomodoro, Flowtime, and self-regulated breaks, so the break schedule matters less than having one. The strongest-evidence micro-rituals are a pre-session if-then plan (implementation intentions, d≈0.65) and a one-minute "ready-to-resume" note when stopping, which reduces attention residue. Leading apps add automation (DND, blocking, music, lights) and post-session reflection.

### Cited Findings
- **Biwer et al. 2023 (Br. J. Educ. Psychol., n=87):** compared self-regulated breaks (n=35) with Pomodoro-style 24/6 breaks (n=25) and 12/3 breaks (n=27). Self-regulated students reported more fatigue and distractedness and less concentration and motivation. Mental effort and task completion didn't differ, so systematic breaks were more efficient (same output in less time). It was a single-day study. — [PubMed 36859717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/); [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/understanding-effort-regulation-comparing-pomodoro-breaks-and-sel)
- **2025 follow-up (Behavioral Sciences, n=94):** self-regulated vs Pomodoro (25/5) vs Flowtime. Pomodoro raised fatigue faster, and Pomodoro and Flowtime lowered motivation faster, but overall fatigue and motivation levels didn't differ between conditions. — [MDPI Behav. Sci. 15(7):861](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/7/861)
- **Implementation intentions:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (94 tests) found d=0.65 on goal attainment. Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2024 (642 tests) found d=0.27–0.66, with larger effects for if-then format, high motivation, and rehearsed plans. — [Konstanz repository](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905); [NCI summary](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/research/constructs/implementation-intentions)
- **Ready-to-resume plan (Leroy & Glomb 2018, Organization Science, four studies):** when interrupted, writing about a minute on where you stopped and what's next reduced attention residue and improved performance on the interrupting task, including better decisions and recall. Most evidence is from the lab. — [UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2018/01/16/task-interrupted-a-plan-for-returning-helps-you-move-on/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180116151600.htm)
- **Session automation:** Session (Mac) blocks sites and apps, mutes Slack, toggles DND, changes bulb color, and plays music, then prompts reflection after each session. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/il/app/id1521432881); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/session)
- **Pause policy:** Forest Plus allows a pause of up to 5 minutes without killing the tree, and the pause can be disabled. Deep Focus has no exit except "Give Up". — [forestapp.cc](https://forestapp.cc/llms-full.txt)
- **Breaks as rituals:** Forest's "Mindful Space" offers breathing and soundscapes between sessions, and Tide has guided 4-7-8 breathing. — [forestapp.cc](https://forestapp.cc/llms-full.txt); [App Store Tide](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1077776989)
- **Interval structure in a space metaphor:** Orbital Focus splits long sessions into "burns" with short "coasts". — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/orbital-focus)
- **Arrival celebration:** a Product Hunt user singled out Orbital Focus's Mars arrival animation as a highlight. — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/orbital-focus)
- **Planning:** Pomofocus estimates pomodoros per task and an estimated finish time. — [pomofocus.io](https://pomofocus.io/)
- **Bugs to avoid:** in the Lofi Girl extension, the long break never triggers. A long-break cycle is something users notice and expect. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/gmkiokemhjjdjmpnnjmnpkpfoenpnpne)

### Inferences
- **Boarding (pre-flight):** pick a destination planet (topic), a task from the todo list, and duration presets (25/5 "short hop", 50/10 "long haul", and Flowtime "open cruise" that runs until you land manually). Add an optional one-line "if distracted, then I will…" field on the boarding pass. Keep it under 10 seconds, with "same as last flight" reuse.
- **In flight:** the timer is visible but can be minimized. A one-tap "log distraction" button ("turbulence") records a thought or URL to a scratchpad without leaving the flight, and on the web, tab-hidden events are logged automatically. Pauses come from a limited "fuel reserve" (for example, one pause of 5 minutes or less), modeled on Forest.
- **Break or landing:** a short arrival moment (2–4 seconds: stamp, planet reveal, chime) and then a prompt with three quick inputs: focus rating (1–5), "what got done?" (auto-checks todos), and "next step" (the ready-to-resume note, prefilled into the next boarding pass). Make it skippable.
- **Break content:** an "observation deck" with calm imagery and optional breathing, plus a gentle prompt to stand up. Avoid sending people back into feeds.
- **Long-break cycle:** after 4 flights, offer a "refuel at station" long break. Make sure it actually triggers (unlike the Lofi Girl bug).
- **DND on the web:** the Notifications API for end cues only, optional fullscreen "cockpit mode", and suggestions to enable OS Focus/DND. True blocking requires a browser extension (Forest ships Chrome/Firefox extensions), which could be a later roadmap item.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence found on focus-quality self-ratings, distraction logging, or arrival celebrations specifically improving outcomes. These are design patterns, not validated interventions.
- No study found comparing 50/10 with 25/5 directly. Brain.fm's "40-minute interval is research-based" claim cites no specific paper.
- The well-known interruption-cost figures (for example, "23 minutes to resume") were not verified in this research pass.

## 5. Gamification for a space theme and Korean market specifics (열품타/YPT, 공부 타이머 culture)

### Takeaway
Korean study-timer culture centers on measuring pure study time (순공시간), live co-presence in groups, and rankings by exam category. 열품타 (YPT) dominates with about 4.8만 ratings at 4.5★ and a #10 Education chart position. Its users are vocal about ranking integrity (manually edited time leaking into rankings), home-screen redesigns that hide the daily total, and to-do ordering bugs. Space gamification is crowded (planets, rockets, stardust). Cosmodoro can stand out with a cumulative solar-system journey tied to topics, passport stamps, and honest, timer-only social stats.

### Inferences
**Prioritized roadmap candidates (inferred from the evidence above)**
- **P0, calm flight and honest timing:** a low-motion cruise view, reduced-motion support, and a solid task panel. Timer-measured minutes are the single source of truth for stats and rankings. Manual corrections are allowed but flagged and excluded from any social or ranking total, which directly answers the YPT complaint.
- **P0, the daily total stays big:** keep "today's focus time" and the per-planet (subject) split prominent on the home screen. YPT's redesign backlash shows users anchor on that number. Offer a "classic or minimal" home layout.
- **P0, boarding → arrival ritual:** a boarding pass with task and if-then intention, a launch countdown, arrival stamp and chime, a 1–5 focus rating, and a ready-to-resume note carried into the next boarding pass.
- **P1, passport and journey:** each topic planet gets a passport page with stamps (date, duration, focus rating). Cumulative focus time converts to distance on a persistent solar-system map, giving "Earth → Moon → Mars → … voyages" across weeks. Milestone reveals (new moons, rings, comets) fit Forest's monthly "signature" arc and Let's Focus's rarity collecting, but stay tied to real minutes.
- **P1, YPT-style heatmap calendar:** day, week, and month cells that darken with study time ("calendar gets darker the more you study"), plus a daily max focus streak and a subject-share breakdown.
- **P1, forgiving streaks:** weekly-target streaks ("5 flights this week") and a limited "fuel reserve" freeze, with no shame copy. This follows Duolingo's freeze rationale and the Smashing Magazine anxiety warnings.
- **P2, crew (group) mode for Korea:** live member status (in flight, on break, docked), group weekly totals, and opt-in "shared stakes" in the style of Forest's Plant Together. Optional exam-category leaderboards (수능, 공시, 어학) count only measured time. Group goal rules (for example, a weekly hour target) are a known Korean group norm.
- **P2, ship cosmetics:** ship skins, cockpit themes, and per-planet ambient sound packs as the main paid layer. No ads during flights. Avoid Habitica-style mini-games that compete with the task.
- **P3, real-world tie-in or school challenges:** these mirror Forest's real trees and Naver Pay's school-vs-school challenge. They suit later partnerships, for example donations or school leagues.
- **Korean localization:** use 순공시간 terminology, D-Day countdowns for exams, 과목 = 행성 mapping, and KRW pricing around YPT's range (₩3,900–5,900/month) as a reference ceiling for a web product.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) official 열품타 MAU or download figures. The 3 million figure is from 2021.
- No Korean articles on 캠스터디 or 스터디윗미 (study-with-me) trends in 2025–2026 surfaced.
- The exact YPT group ranking rules (period, tie-breaks, anti-cheat) weren't documented in accessible sources.
- No evidence on whether space or planet collectibles improve retention compared with other metaphors. All space-app evidence here is listing copy, not outcome data.
